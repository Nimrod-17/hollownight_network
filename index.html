Post 2

Network Stats

Every figure below is computed live, in the browser, straight from the same data.json the graph above uses — nothing here is hand-typed, so it stays accurate as the dataset grows. A short plain-language note sits next to anything that isn't obvious.

Most connected

The ten entries linked to the most others - the hubs nearly everyone else eventually passes through.

    Least connected

    The ten entries with the fewest links - easy to miss, easy to forget, including any with none at all.

      Forgotten fragments

      Connected components: groups of entries only reachable from each other, cut off from the rest of the graph. The largest is the "giant component."

      Clustering

      Average clustering coefficient - on average, how likely two of an entry's own connections are to also be connected to each other.

      Distances

      Average shortest path length, and the diameter (the single longest shortest path in the graph) - how many hops separate any two entries.

      Hub share

      What share of every link in the graph touches just the single most-connected entry.

      The friendship paradox

      A well-known network quirk: on average, an entry's own connections have more connections than it does - because hubs get counted as "someone's connection" far more often than as themselves.

      Degree distribution

      How many entries have exactly N connections. A roughly straight line on the log-log scale is the signature of a "scale-free" network: a few extreme hubs, many barely-connected entries.
